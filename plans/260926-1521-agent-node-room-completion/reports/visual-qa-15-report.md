- **Harness confounds (disclosed).**
  - **Connection-pool starvation in the first two recovered-Abandon checks.** In O1 and O2 the survivor and fresh tabs shared one browser context with the driver pages. Every Legacy and Console page holds a long-lived EventSource, and Chromium allows 6 connections per host per context. At 6 streams per host, every REST read queued: 0 GETs from any of the 8 tabs in the 30 s after the Abandon (`data/O2-recab.json`, `gets`). That data is discarded. From O3 on, every survivor and fresh tab has its own context. With that change, recovered Abandons show `Failed` at 26–1026 ms (below). The O1 and O2 survivor-outage frames (4 tabs plus 4 driver pages, under the limit) and their cold loads (own contexts) are kept.
  - **Withdraw probe timing.** My first probe helper clicked the observer's `delete ·` a fixed delay after the Send now **click**, not after the POST resolved. For Claude (`cl`) both probe clicks lost the race (`removed:false`). For Codex (`cx`) the c2 probe won the race: the withdraw committed 3 ms before the Send now resolved (`removed:true`), the node stayed idle with an empty queue, and the rest of that cycle stalled. I stopped that chain by exact PID, Abandoned the run, fixed the probe to arm after the send resolves (the round-14 method), and ran Codex again as `cx2`. The race itself is characterised below (Observations).
  - **Disk and memory pauses.** Free disk fell to 1.5 GB and then to 222 MB (ENOSPC). The cause was macOS swap, not my files; my scratch dir stayed at about 150 MB. ENOSPC killed my Vite server on 5294 at 13:01. I paused at the coordinator's request, stopped my drivers and Vite by exact PID to lower memory, and restarted them after 109 GB was free again (14:04). The I2 idle watcher kept running through the pause and recorded the failure (below), so I2 did not need a re-run. Measurements in progress were not split across the pause: the pause fell between batches.
